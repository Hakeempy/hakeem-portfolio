## The problem

An e-commerce company needed a database for its products, customers and orders that keeps stock correct
without anyone updating it by hand. Placing an order has to reduce stock for every product in it, every
stock change has to be recorded, low stock has to be easy to spot, and the business wants summaries of
orders and of how much each customer spends.

## How it works

1. Five tables hold the data: `Products` (with stock and a reorder level), `Customers` (with a unique
   email), `Orders`, `Order_Details` (one row per product per order, keyed on both) and
   `Inventory_Logs`. Foreign keys tie them together, and a check constraint stops stock going below
   zero.
2. `PlaceMultipleOrder` takes a customer ID and two comma-separated lists, product IDs and quantities.
   Inside a transaction it creates the order, works through the lists, looks up each price, applies a
   quantity discount, writes the order lines, reduces stock, and finally records the order's total
   amount and item count. If any line fails, for example because stock would go below zero, the whole
   order rolls back and the error is raised.
3. The trigger `trg_log_inventory_change` fires after any update to a product's stock and writes the
   difference to `Inventory_Logs`, labelled "Order Placement" or "Stock Replenishment".
4. `ReplenishStock` adds stock back, and the same trigger logs it.
5. Three views answer the reporting questions. `OrderSummary` shows each order with its customer and
   totals, `LowStockProducts` lists everything below its reorder level, and `CustomerTiers` ranks
   customers by total spend: Gold from 1,000, Silver from 500, Bronze below that.

## Decisions worth explaining

- **Logging in a trigger, not in the procedures.** Every stock change, whether from an order, a restock
  or a manual update, passes through the same trigger, so the log cannot be forgotten.
- **Prices frozen at order time.** `Order_Details` stores the price when the order was placed and the
  discounted price, so a later price change does not rewrite old orders.
- **One discount rule.** 2% from three items, 5% from five and 10% from ten, decided in the procedure so
  every order gets the same rule.
- **Views for reporting.** Each reporting question becomes a one-line query, and the logic behind it
  lives in one place.
- **A test script.** `Testing.sql` places an order, then checks the order, its lines, the new stock
  levels, the log, each view, and a restock.

## What I would change next

I would check stock before deducting it, so a short order gets a clear message, and let a scheduled event
raise a restock request for everything in `LowStockProducts`, since restocking is still manual.
